# Arabic (`ar`)

This file is read by Arabic speakers across the Arab world who use Infinitary, most of whom have never opened a terminal.
The register is calm Modern Standard Arabic as a careful colleague writes it, with no dialect words, never cute and never salesy.
The person is addressed in the masculine singular where grammar forces a choice, as Apple's Arabic does, and labels, buttons and commands use a verbal noun ("فتح الإعدادات", "إغلاق البطاقة") so most of them need no gender at all.
A feed line about a task agrees with "مهمة" in the feminine ("دُمجت", "توقفت"), and a line about a change, a project or a crewmate stays masculine ("دُمج", "أُنشئ").
Punctuation uses the Arabic comma "،", semicolon "؛" and question mark "؟", quotation marks are « », and the text carries no direction marks because the window sets the direction.
Numbers reach the messages as digits from the runtime, a unit follows the number with a space ("1.6 غ.ب", "قبل 5 د"), and every plural form keeps {count} with the noun in the case Arabic number agreement asks for: two takes the dual, three to ten the plural, eleven to ninety-nine the singular accusative, and zero, one and a hundred or more the singular.
