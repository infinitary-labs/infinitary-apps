# Infinitary Strategy

## Vision

<!-- vision: author-approved 2026-11-22 -->

`Infinitary` exists to enable every individual to easily leverage the full power of AI to achieve their ambition, with a studio of their own.
It serves the captain: one person with more to do than attention to give, who may or may not have a professional technical background.
It turns what they ask once into work that is made with care, shown with its evidence and kept on record.
The feeling it aims for is calm: confidence that the work is in hand, yet they remain in control of their vision and important decisions.
It owns exactly one thing: what the captain sees, decides and keeps between their words and the crew's work.

### One first mate, and the machinery stays hidden

The captain talks to one first mate, by typing or by speaking; a crewmate asks for them only through a card.
One first mate serves one captain, also when that captain works within a team: what the team shares reaches each person through their own first mate.
Crewmates are never a focal point: how they work is one click deeper, never in the way.
Harnesses, transports, routing and sessions are machinery; the app speaks of providers, accounts and allowances and nothing below them.
The app speaks plainly, so that someone who has never opened a terminal can follow it and a technical person never feels talked down to; a technical name appears where the captain chose it, where it is the thing itself, or one click deeper.
Nothing the captain may need is deliberately kept from them: what is not on the surface - how a crewmate worked, the changed code, what a task cost - can be one click deeper, never deliberately kept absent.
The fewer concepts the captain must learn, the better: a capability is first something to ask the first mate for, and a control only where the captain's standing choice is the point.
The app does not teach itself with a tour: each feature is discoverable at the right moment and in the right place, and anything can also be found by asking.
The main window is mostly empty when nothing needs the captain, with no cheering, no token counts, no cost figures and no finish estimates: more on the surface is information overload, and overload ends the calm.
The first mate and crew are drawn as figures whose light and motion report real state - hot while a turn runs, cool when idle, frozen when out of reach - and never guessed activity.

### Cards are the default

Work comes back as cards: a decision to make, or a result to see.
A card exists only when work cannot move without the captain's decision, or the captain cannot move without a fact; progress, retries and waits belong to Activity, never to a card.
A card leads with its answer and draws what it can; long text folds behind it.
A new card never takes the place of the one being read, and no card goes unseen.
Every card is a thread with its author: the captain replies in place, and a reply decides nothing by itself.
A conversation view may exist for looking back, but it is never the default way to work: a stream does not scale when much work is happening, and the card stack is designed for exactly that.

### The captain decides, the fleet does the work

Whatever reaches the captain leaves them only what the fleet cannot do: choose what they want, give a consent, sign in, or reach a device.
The state it aims for is high velocity: the captain says what they want and decides what comes back, and the first mate arranges the work, so tending tasks by hand is never their part.
Their part is one click wherever one click can carry it, and every option offered is one they would seriously consider.
A choice arrives unpicked: the app marks what it recommends and never picks for them.
Nothing merges, spends past a limit, uses a secret, moves the first mate or submits the final step on a web page without the captain's yes.
A standing yes - autonomy for a project, an action always allowed - is a grant the captain makes; it is never a default and never widens by itself.
A grant names the action it allows: nothing grants a project or a crew trust in general, because that would be trust in whichever model runs there.
The first mate starts no work the captain did not ask for unless they turned that on: unasked work spends their allowance, and that trade is theirs alone.
A yes is kept and served to the same work, so the captain is asked once.
Evidence is not consent: passing checks, a review or a recommendation authorize nothing.

### Made with care, shown and recorded

Each task belongs to one crewmate in its own working copy, so work never collides.
A result arrives with its evidence: what changed, a picture of each changed view before and after, and the checks that ran.
A claim that was not checked says so, and a failed read never shows as an empty or a passing one.
Every task keeps a record: the captain's words verbatim, the plan, each report, each card and what was pressed on it, the change and its checks.
The record is durable and the captain's to read; only their own act deletes it.

### Yours

The first mate lives on a computer the captain chooses: one they own, or one run for them.
Either way it stays theirs: their own computers can keep a copy of everything, and the first mate moves to any computer they own whenever they choose.
On the captain's own computers the app needs no account of ours, and no feature there waits on sending us anything.
Model use runs on the captain's own account with the provider they chose and goes straight there; the app never sits between, meters or resells it.
The captain's other computers and phone join the first mate's computer directly, and between the captain's own devices a service of ours carries only what it cannot read.
What a build reports about itself is anonymous and never the captain's words, is told at first launch and in Settings, and a build made from this source reports nothing.
An agent never sees a secret's value.
Directions, knowledge, runbooks and panels are the captain's to read and change.

### One first mate's computer, every other surface a view of it

A first mate lives on one computer; every other computer, iPhone and iPad is a view of it or a place its crew works, and none becomes a second first mate for the same work.
One person may be part of many unrelated projects or businesses, so the app on any device can switch between several first mates, each with its own record, knowledge and secrets.
Infinitary on iPhone and iPad adds no feature the desktop app lacks: it may adapt how things work and where they sit, and anything new there needs the captain's deliberate decision first.
What one surface can do, every surface does the same way: every feature works from the keyboard, every word ships in every language the app speaks, the first mate and crew look and move the same everywhere, and an integration serves the first mate, a crewmate, a schedule and a panel alike.
Closing the window ends nothing: the first mate and crew keep working while their computer is awake.

### Arranged before built

A new capability is arranged from what the app already has - tasks, cards, the record, runbooks, panels, schedules, integrations - before anything new is built.
What a feature knows lives in words the captain can read and rewrite, so a variation is a sentence to the first mate, not a release.
The app has no modes: people with different work start with a different set of built-in runbooks turned on, and the runbooks shape the rest of the experience.
Runbooks may be written by other people and gathered in one central place; the captain adds each one by choice, knowing who wrote it and what it reaches, and every update asks again.
The app builds the agents' reach and never their verdicts: it holds typed facts, consents and physical limits, and leaves judgment to the agent and the choice to the captain.
Work beyond code - a question answered, a web page driven while the captain watches, a schedule, a lesson, a panel - travels through the same first mate, cards and record, never through a second product inside the app.

### Scope

Infinitary is not a chat app, not a code editor, not a terminal or session manager, not a dashboard for watching agents, and not a model provider.
This vision covers what the apps do for the person using them, on the desktop and on iPhone and iPad; pricing and the company are not decided here.
The project holds itself to its own standard: a change to it arrives with its evidence and merges on the captain's yes or a grant they made.

A change aligns when it gives the captain more finished, proven work for the same attention; lets them move faster; removes a concept, a click or a word they must handle; makes a decision clearer or a result more visible; keeps their words and work theirs to copy, move and delete; or reaches every surface at once.
A change should be resisted when it adds a concept or a mode the captain must learn, puts on the surface what they need not act on or deliberately keeps from them what they may need, asks them to arrange work by hand, makes a stream the default way to work, reports progress as news, decides, starts or spends for the captain without a grant, grants trust in general instead of a named action, shows a result without evidence or closes work without a record, sends the captain's words anywhere they did not choose, gives one surface a feature the others lack, or builds a second way to do what a primitive already does.
