---
title: What you can ask for
description: The kinds of asks your first mate and your crew take on, with an example of each and what comes back.
---

You ask in your own words, and your first mate decides how to take it on.
This page shows the kinds of asks it knows, with one example each.
You never need to name the kind.

## A change to a project

"On the website, make the sign-up button easier to see on phones."

A crewmate makes the change in its own working copy of your project.
When it is checked, the crewmate brings a merge card with the change's story.
See [Your first task](/your-first-task/).

For something broken, say what you saw, such as "The Save button on the settings page does nothing."
A crewmate first tries to make the problem happen.
If it cannot, your first mate asks you before anyone tries a fix.
The fix comes with a check that failed before the change and passes after it.

## A question answered

"How does the sign-up page decide which plan to show?"

Your first mate answers a quick question itself.
When the answer needs a look through your project, a crewmate reads it and brings the answer as a card, with where each part came from.
Reply under the card to ask more, and press **Done** when you have what you need.
See [Cards and decisions](/cards-and-decisions/).

## A plan before building

"Plan a dark mode for the website, and show me before you build it."

A crewmate reads your project and brings the plan as a card.
It asks only the decisions that are yours to make, and says what it decided itself.
**Approve and build** starts the work, and **Save and defer** keeps the plan for later.

Ask for a prototype or a mock-up, and the card holds a preview you can try and adjust before anything is built.
Your first mate also plans first on its own when an ask is unclear or touches several screens.

## Research

"Compare three ways to add search to the website, with what each would cost."

Research looks past your project: on the web, or by running and measuring something.
The answer comes as a card with its sources, and keeps what is established apart from what is guessed.

## A schedule

"Every weekday at 9 AM, tell me which pull requests are waiting on me."

Your first mate makes an automation: a prompt it gets on a schedule, or each time something happens.
See [Automation](/automation/).

## A panel

"Make me a panel that shows the weather in Tokyo."

A crewmate builds a panel for the side of the window, from the app's own parts.
See [Panels](/panels/#panels-your-first-mate-builds-for-you).

## A subject learned across days

"Teach me chess, a little each day."

Each subject is one task that stays open across days.
Its first card asks what you want to be able to do.
Each round then comes as a lesson card of 8 to 12 items.
Press **Next round** for more, or **Stop for today**.

The crewmate keeps track of what you got right and what you mixed up, and opens each sitting with a short review.
A skill counts as learned only when you get it right again on a later day.
After your first finished round, the subject gets a panel with your goal and your practice days.

## A small project for fun

"Make me a small game where a cat catches falling fish."

Your first mate sets it up as a project of its own, on a card where you press **Add project**.
It proposes keeping the project on this computer and letting finished work merge without asking.
The crewmate builds it as a plain web page that opens with no install.
It plays the game first, then brings a note with a picture and an **Open** button.

## A look at a computer under strain

"My computer is running slow. Can you check what finished tasks left running?"

When a computer warns that it is under strain, your first mate offers this look on a card.
The look only reads.
It lists what each finished task left running, and other programs with what stopping them would cost.
It stops only the ones you pick, and it uses your plan's allowance.

## A job kept running

"Back up my Documents folder to my external drive every night."

A crewmate sets up the job with your computer's own scheduler, so it keeps running after the task closes.
Its card names the job, the computer it runs on, when it runs, how to check it and how to remove it.
You find it later under **Kept running** in Settings → **Automation**.
See [Automation](/automation/#kept-running).

## The guidance behind these

Your first mate follows built-in guidance, called runbooks, for some of these kinds: **Common workflows**, **Lessons**, **Casual projects** and **Computer cleanup**.
You can read each one or turn it off in Settings → **Runbooks**.
See [Runbooks](/runbooks/).
